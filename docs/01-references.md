# Canver — Map of references

**Status:** initial exploration (03-sep-2026)
**Seed idea:** a platform where *you can only publish by answering* another post. The threads read as one continuous text — a story the world builds. Inspired by the folded-paper game (exquisite corpse). Constraint: a limited number of answers per period; you choose which story to continue.

---

## 1. Map of references

### A. Direct lineage: digital chained narrative

| Reference | Core mechanic | What to learn |
|---|---|---|
| **Ficlets** (AOL, 2006–2009) | Stories of 64–1024 bytes. Anyone could write a *prequel* or a *sequel* to any ficlet → real branching. CC-BY-SA. ~49k stories, 10k members in 2 years. | The closest model to the idea. Two-way branching is richer than "continue" alone. It died of corporate abandonment, not of a missing community. |
| **Ficly** (2009–2014, independent successor) | Same model, made by the founder of Ficlets. 30k+ stories in 5 years. | Died of technical debt and the founder's personal capacity (trigger: Google shut down OpenID). Infrastructure and maintenance kill more projects than a lack of users. |
| **FoldingStory** (2010–active) | Each person writes **one line** and only sees the previous fold. Live sessions, star ratings. | Hiding the context produces the absurd. But it produces *jokes*, not *literature*. On top of that: visually they are loose posts that do not connect. |
| **Protagonize** (2007–~2016) | Branching collaborative writing by chapters. | It turned into a writers' forum more than a game. Genre weighs a lot. |
| **StoryChain / Tale / ShareStory** (2024–2026) | Story by invitation, one sentence per person, AI closing. | The space is populated by private toys among friends, not by a public platform with a shared canon. There is a gap there. |

### B. Visual / non-textual chains

| Reference | Mechanic | What to learn |
|---|---|---|
| **Drawception / Gartic Phone** | Telephone game: phrase → drawing → phrase. Closed rounds, reveal at the end. | The chain works better when it alternates media and when there is a *moment of revelation*. |
| **Exquisite corpse (the surrealists)** | Formal constraint plus partial concealment. | The constraint is not a product limitation: it is the aesthetic generator. |
| **Renga (Japanese chained poetry, 12th c.)** | Rule of *link and shift*: each verse connects to the previous one and moves away from the one two back. | It solves the coherence/drift tension that no digital platform solved. It is the formal heart of the system. |

### C. Social mechanics: constraint and scarcity

| Reference | Mechanic | What to learn |
|---|---|---|
| **BeReal** | One post a day, in a forced window. | Scarcity produces different behaviour, but when it is the only concept it runs out. It needs to rest on an artefact. |
| **Branch** (2012, bought by Facebook) | Public conversation as content in itself. | An exact precedent for "the comment is the content". It failed because manual curation does not scale. |
| **Are.na / Cosmos** | Creative communities with no vanity metrics. | A reference for tone. Are.na charges a subscription: a viable model without advertising. |
| **SCP Wiki / r/place** | Collective canon with hard rules. | A crowd will sustain a coherent work if the rules are strict and visible. |

---

## 2. Cross-cutting readings

1. **Nobody occupies the gap today.** What exists is a private toy among friends, a writers' community, or a generic social network with threads. A public platform where the only possible action is continuing someone else's work is not taken.
2. **All the predecessors died of infrastructure, not of concept.** Ficlets: corporate abandonment. Ficly: technical debt and a single maintainer. Branch: acquisition. The concept was never proven wrong.
3. **The constraint is the product.** Without a limit on answers this is a forum. With a limit, every answer is an editorial decision: *which story do I give my one move to today?*
4. **Central tension: legibility versus drift.** More context = more coherent and more boring; less context = more absurd and less legible as a single text.
